    How Amog reads a game

    \begin{definition}Under normal play a game $G$ falls into one of four outcome classes: positive ($G > 0$: Left wins whoever starts), negative ($G < 0$: Right wins), zero ($G = 0$: the second player wins) and fuzzy ($G \parallel 0$: the first player wins).\end{definition}

    \begin{definition}$G \le H$ when no Left option $G^L$ has $G^L \ge H$ and no Right option $H^R$ has $H^R \le G$. Then $G \ge 0$ means Left wins when Right starts, and $G \le 0$ means Right wins when Left starts.\end{definition}

    Sums: in $G + H$ you move in exactly one component, so $G + H = \{G^L + H, G + H^L \mid G^R + H, G + H^R\}$. Negation swaps the players, $-G = \{-G^R \mid -G^L\}$, and comparing $G$ with $H$ is the same as asking for the outcome of $G - H$: that's how the relations table is filled in.

    \begin{theorem}[Canonical form]Every short game equals a unique simplest game, found by deleting dominated options (a Left option no bigger than another; dually for Right) and bypassing reversible ones (a Left option $A$ with some $A^R \le G$ gets replaced by $A^R$'s Left options; dually for Right).\end{theorem}

    So two games are equal exactly when their canonical forms agree, which is how the library boxes them. Amog names the familiar ones: numbers like $\tfrac34 = \{\tfrac12 \mid 1\}$, nimbers $*n$ (from Nim), $\uparrow = \{0 \mid *\}$ and its multiples, $\pm x = \{x \mid -x\}$, tiny and miny, and sums like $1*$. Everything else prints as nested braces.

    \begin{example}$\uparrow$ is positive, yet smaller than every positive number, and confused with $*$: $\uparrow \parallel *$ but $\uparrow + * = \{0, * \mid 0\}$ is fuzzy while $\uparrow + \uparrow + * = \{0 \mid \uparrow\}$ is positive. Drag them onto the field and see.\end{example}

    Amog only does a quick search by default, so huge sums may show a ?. "Think harder" runs a longer search in the background, for up to a minute, on that game or relation.
