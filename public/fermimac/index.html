How to be roughly right

Feynman and Sharp ask math questions: powers, roots, logs, trig, factorials, primes and friends. Your guess counts if it's within 10% (Feynman) or 2% (Sharp) of the true value.

MathGuessr and FermiGuessr are played like GeoGuessr: ten questions against the clock (math problems, or questions about the world, from the distance to the Moon to the number of ants on Earth), and every guess scores points for how close it is. For answers up to $1$ that's $5000 \times \frac{\text{smaller}}{\text{larger}}$ of your guess and the truth: spot on is 5,000, off by a factor of 2 is 2,500, and off by 10 is 500, so a perfect game is 50,000. Finish all ten early and you keep your time; run out and the rest score nothing.

Big answers get some slack, because they're harder: being off by 3 on the mass of the Earth is a better guess than being off by 3 on the area of your skin, and 10% of $\binom{31}{17}$ is harder to hit than 10% of $\ln 99999$. An answer $a > 1$ has slack $s = 1 + \tfrac{1}{15}\log_{10} a$. The tolerance in Feynman and Sharp is multiplied by $s$, and the Guessr score is $5000 \times \left(\frac{\text{smaller}}{\text{larger}}\right)^{1/s}$. So $\binom{31}{17} \approx 2.7 \times 10^8$ gets $s \approx 1.56$, about 16% in Feynman, and the mass of the Earth gets $s \approx 2.65$: off by a factor of 2 still scores 3,850.

Some tools that help:

  • Logs: $\ln 2 \approx 0.693$, $\ln 10 \approx 2.303$, $\log_{10} 2 \approx 0.301$, $\log_{10} 3 \approx 0.477$. So $e^{x} = 10^{x / 2.303}$, and $2^{10} \approx 10^3$.
  • Primes: $\pi(N) \approx N / \ln N$ (a bit low; $N / (\ln N - 1)$ is better), and the $n$-th prime is about $n (\ln n + \ln \ln n - 1)$.
  • $1 + \tfrac12 + \cdots + \tfrac1n \approx \ln n + 0.577$, and $F_n \approx \varphi^n / \sqrt5$ with $\varphi \approx 1.618$.
  • Stirling: $n! \approx \sqrt{2 \pi n}\,(n/e)^n$. Compound growth: money at $r\%$ doubles in about $72 / r$ years.
  • Small angles: $\sin x \approx x$ in radians, and $1^\circ \approx 0.01745$.
  • Fermi questions: split into factors you know (people × cups per person × …) and multiply. Errors partly cancel.

Fermi answers are rounded values from the mid-2020s.
